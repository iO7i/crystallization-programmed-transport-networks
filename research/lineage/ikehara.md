# Verified scientific lineage

This is a compact literature record, not an institutional affiliation statement.

## Published work located in the audit

- Ikehara, Kimura, and Kataoka (2010), *Miscibility enhancement and formation of
  interpenetrating spherulites in ternary blends of crystalline polymers*, reports
  interpenetrating spherulites in PBS/PES/POE blends and describes sequential and
  simultaneous crystallization processes. DOI: [10.1002/polb.21940](https://doi.org/10.1002/polb.21940).
- Ikehara and Kataoka (2018), *The degree of crystallinity and segmental mobility in
  interpenetrating spherulites of poly(butylene succinate) and poly(ethylene oxide)*,
  examines component crystallinity and segmental mobility using pulsed NMR. DOI:
  [10.1038/s41428-018-0029-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41428-018-0029-7).
- Ikehara, Kurihara, and Kataoka (2012), *Spherulitic growth in block copolymers and
  blends of miscible crystalline polymers*, compares PES/PEO-related crystalline
  block-copolymer and blend behavior. DOI: [10.1002/polb.23036](https://doi.org/10.1002/polb.23036).
- Ikehara and Kataoka (2009), *Interpenetration between Spherulites of the Same
  Component in Polymer Blends*, provides an open J-STAGE record of the interpenetration
  phenomenon. DOI: [10.1295/koron.66.450](https://doi.org/10.1295/koron.66.450).

The broader crystallizable-block-copolymer literature supplies the confinement
comparison. Loo, Register, and Ryan (2002) distinguish breakout, templated, and
confined crystallization in block-copolymer microdomains (DOI:
[10.1021/ma011824j](https://doi.org/10.1021/ma011824j)). Alharbe, Register, and Hobbs
(2017) report orientation-dependent confined, templated, and breakout crystallization
in a soft-confined cylindrical system (DOI:
[10.1021/acs.macromol.6b02361](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.macromol.6b02361)).

## Personal connection, stated narrowly

Hosam Al-Khairat conducted undergraduate chemical-engineering research under Professor
Takayuki Ikehara on crystallization behavior in a PES-b-PEO-related system. This
repository returns to that family of physical questions using computational methods;
it does not use old unpublished measurements as validated input data.

This project is independent and does not represent Kanagawa University or the Ikehara
Laboratory. The cited researchers are not repository contributors, advisors,
collaborators, or endorsers unless they later become involved and explicitly authorize
that representation.
